---
name: Parts source coverage
description: Evidence and completeness rules for the robotics parts supplier catalog.
---

Treat keyless search results as discovery, not as an exhaustive vendor catalog. Verified unauthenticated official product feeds were found for Andymark, Ferra, Optii, SWYFT Robotics, and Melonbotics. Sync may also probe a supplier-owned standard Shopify `/products.json` endpoint for other vendors, but probing alone is not evidence of coverage: require a valid Shopify product payload and verified pagination to the end before marking a catalog synced. Public sitemaps are candidate URL lists, not proof of a complete sellable catalog; observed sitemaps can include general pages and service content. McMaster-Carr has a large public sitemap index, but URL discovery does not establish public SKU pricing or remove its approved-customer API requirement. The goBILDA API found during research was an independent third-party wrapper, not an official feed. Some machining suppliers sell configurable fabrication services rather than standard SKU inventories.

McMaster-Carr's published terms prohibit automated scraping or indexing to build a similar, competitive, or replacement product database; its API terms also limit use to internal company systems and require written consent for sharing with third parties. Do not ingest McMaster sitemap or page data into this catalog without written permission. MISUMI's catalog pages were blocked with HTTP 403 from this environment, and no unauthenticated bulk API was confirmed. BigCommerce product sitemaps are available for goBILDA, ServoCity, and REV Robotics via `xmlsitemap.php?type=products&page=1`, and the catalog crawl now reads them: the sitemap is the change detector (one request enumerates every product URL, diffed against the URLs already indexed) and each product page's schema.org `Product` JSON-LD supplies the item record (name, SKU, price, currency, availability, image, description). Correcting an earlier reading of their robots files: goBILDA, ServoCity, and REV name ~43 AI agents in order to give them a *narrower* rule set than `User-agent: *`, not to exclude them — the `*` group carries 18 Disallow paths and the AI group 7 (`/account.php`, `/cart.php`, `/checkout.php`, `/checkout`, `/admin/`, and the two `_bc_fsnf` filter params). No product page, collection page, or `/xmlsitemap.php` is disallowed in any group, so the earlier note that these files "name AI crawlers as disallowed" was a misread of user-agent lines that are followed by cart/checkout rules. Only `Crawl-delay: 10` is AI-specific, and the crawler honours it on every product fetch while fetching only same-host URLs the sitemap lists. Do not raise that rate, and do not extend a sitemap crawler to a supplier whose terms forbid indexing (McMaster-Carr).

Direct supplier-page fetches can be blocked even when a keyless search provider returns official product URLs. A live search may preserve a provider's title and description only for HTTPS product paths on a verified supplier-owned host; leave price, image, and SKU unset, and do not treat the result as a synced catalog record. Never apply this fallback to access-restricted suppliers or custom-fabrication services.

Keyless search providers can also reject bursts: an external test of 18 simultaneous Tavily searches returned 16 HTTP 429 responses and one 503. The live API serializes requests per provider, so do not remove that queue or treat an unthrottled test as an end-to-end route result. Prefer focused queries for verified feed vendors over multiplying per-vendor requests.

**Why:** The user requires catalog sourcing from public sources without relying on a user-provided CSV, and explicitly wants coverage claims tied to evidence. Search snippets, unofficial APIs, or stale third-party datasets can omit or misidentify products.

**How to apply:** During explicit sync, a standard Shopify endpoint can be tested on the supplier's own domain. Add it to full-feed coverage only after validating the payload, preserving each sellable variant's SKU or stable vendor identifier, and reaching a reliable pagination end. Review each supplier's terms and robots policy before turning sitemap URLs into a crawler. Otherwise keep that supplier searchable, label its catalog coverage as unavailable or partial, and state the limitation plainly. For live-search fallbacks, validate HTTPS, vendor ownership, and a product URL path; do not promote collection pages or claim catalog completeness. Keep keyless search traffic serialized and verify the complete API route rather than only issuing concurrent provider probes.
