---
name: Parts source coverage
description: Evidence and completeness rules for the robotics parts supplier catalog.
---

Treat keyless search results as discovery, not as an exhaustive vendor catalog. Verified unauthenticated official product feeds were found for Andymark, Ferra, Optii, SWYFT Robotics, Melonbotics, and WestCoast Products. The products.json feeds also carry per-variant prices; the collector stores them using each vendor's configured feed price currency (USD for the US stores, AUD for optii.com.au) because products.json never states a currency. WestCoast Products (wcproducts.com, Shopify) is not to be trusted on its own products.json — its variant data is stale for store-hidden SKU pages, and its PDP configurator can show "Sold Out"/"BackOrdered" for options the feed lists as available. But the store also publishes a UCP catalog API at /api/ucp/mcp (announced in /.well-known/ucp, plus an agents.md invite for agents) — that is the supplier-sanctioned machine-readable path. A full browse of it returns ~1000 purchasable products plus ~41 out-of-stock ones (pass `filters.available:false`; a second dual-pass browse is required or the union is incomplete) ≈ the ~1041 currently sellable. The products.json feed (2135 entries) contains everything they sell, including ~550 store-hidden price-list SKU pages the sitemap and the UCP browse list omit — auditing all 551 of them with one .js request each (paced, retrying on 429) found every one live (wcp-0909/wcp-1849/kit-0106/wcp-2419 all orderable). Design shipped: products.json is the completeness backbone (feedPriceCurrency fills in the unspecified currency), the dual-pass UCP browse overlays live per-variant price/currency/availability keyed by `productId:variantId` onto the feed records each sync (the deployment discovers its own public origin from incoming request headers and serves the platform agent profile itself at `/ucp-agent-profile.json` — zero deployment config, works on any domain), stock_status column drives an in-stock/out-of-stock badge. The supplier rate-limits hard (~1-2 req/s sustained; bursts get 429s, which once masqueraded as 404s). Sync may also probe a supplier-owned standard Shopify `/products.json` endpoint for other vendors, but probing alone is not evidence of coverage: require a valid Shopify product payload and verified pagination to the end before marking a catalog synced. Public sitemaps are candidate URL lists, not proof of a complete sellable catalog; observed sitemaps can include general pages and service content. The goBILDA API found during research was an independent third-party wrapper, not an official feed.

BigCommerce product sitemaps are available for goBILDA, ServoCity, and REV Robotics via `xmlsitemap.php?type=products&page=1`, and the catalog crawl now reads them: the sitemap is the change detector (one request enumerates every product URL, diffed against the URLs already indexed) and each product page's schema.org `Product` JSON-LD supplies the item record (name, SKU, price, currency, availability, image, description). Correcting an earlier reading of their robots files: goBILDA, ServoCity, and REV name ~43 AI agents in order to give them a *narrower* rule set than `User-agent: *`, not to exclude them — the `*` group carries 18 Disallow paths and the AI group 7 (`/account.php`, `/cart.php`, `/checkout.php`, `/checkout`, `/admin/`, and the two `_bc_fsnf` filter params). No product page, collection page, or `/xmlsitemap.php` is disallowed in any group, so the earlier note that these files "name AI crawlers as disallowed" was a misread of user-agent lines that are followed by cart/checkout rules. Only `Crawl-delay: 10` is AI-specific, and the crawler honours it on every product fetch while fetching only same-host URLs the sitemap lists. Do not raise that rate, and do not extend a sitemap crawler to a supplier whose terms forbid indexing.

Direct supplier-page fetches can be blocked even when a keyless search provider returns official product URLs. A live search may preserve a provider's title and description only for HTTPS product paths on a verified supplier-owned host; leave price, image, and SKU unset, and do not treat the result as a synced catalog record. Apply this fallback only to suppliers whose terms permit indexing; keep others searchable only.

Wix stores expose a flat `<urlset>` product sitemap (`store-products-sitemap.xml`) and a complete schema.org `Product` JSON-LD block on every product page, which is what makes them crawlable like the BigCommerce suppliers. Two Wix JSON-LD shapes differ from BigCommerce and the crawl parser handles both: no top-level `url` on the Product block (the `offers.url` carries the absolute page URL) and `image` as an array of ImageObject entries (or plain URL strings) instead of one string. Platypus Robotics (`platypus-robotics.org`, a goBILDA/REV/Misumi reseller) is the template case: its product sitemap enumerated 274 URLs — matching the storefront's own "274 products" count — all 274 pages yielded a Product record in Nov 2026 (269 with SKU, all USD, 247 in stock) into `platypusRoboticsCatalogSeed.ts`, and it is registered with `productSitemapUrl` so the sitemap diff picks up new listings after the seed bootstrap. Its robots.txt disallows nothing except `*?lightbox=`; only dotbot/AhrefsBot carry `Crawl-delay: 10`, which the shared crawler constant already honours. Wix JSON-LD descriptions are URL blobs, not prose, so seeds for Wix vendors synthesize the description from SKU/price/stock/brand rather than copying the blob.

Keyless search providers can also reject bursts: an external test of 18 simultaneous Tavily searches returned 16 HTTP 429 responses and one 503. The live API serializes requests per provider, so do not remove that queue or treat an unthrottled test as an end-to-end route result. Prefer focused queries for verified feed vendors over multiplying per-vendor requests.

**Why:** The user requires catalog sourcing from public sources without relying on a user-provided CSV, and explicitly wants coverage claims tied to evidence. Search snippets, unofficial APIs, or stale third-party datasets can omit or misidentify products.

**How to apply:** During explicit sync, a standard Shopify endpoint can be tested on the supplier's own domain. Add it to full-feed coverage only after validating the payload, preserving each sellable variant's SKU or stable vendor identifier, and reaching a reliable pagination end. Review each supplier's terms and robots policy before turning sitemap URLs into a crawler. Otherwise keep that supplier searchable, label its catalog coverage as unavailable or partial, and state the limitation plainly. For live-search fallbacks, validate HTTPS, vendor ownership, and a product URL path; do not promote collection pages or claim catalog completeness. Keep keyless search traffic serialized and verify the complete API route rather than only issuing concurrent provider probes.
